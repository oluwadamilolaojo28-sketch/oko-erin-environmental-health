What is environmental health?

Environmental health looks at how the places where we live, work, learn and trade affect our health. Clean water, safe waste disposal, clear drains and hygienic food are simple things that stop sickness before it starts. When neighbours and environmental health personnel work together, everyone stays healthier.

ECO-SAN · ECOLOGICAL SANITATION

Rethinking Sanitation. Protecting Our Environment.

Ecological sanitation offers ways to manage human waste safely while conserving water and protecting the environment. Learn how suitable sanitation systems can support healthier homes and communities.

Illustration: a simple scene of homes, trees, a small sanitation facility and a protected well. A full painted illustration can replace it.

What is ECO-SAN?

ECO-SAN is short for ecological sanitation. It is not just toilet cleaning. It is a way of designing sanitation so that human waste is contained and treated safely, the environment is protected, water is saved where the design allows it, and, in some systems, useful nutrients are recovered after proper treatment.

Why it exists

Poorly managed waste can pollute water and soil and spread disease. ECO-SAN approaches aim to prevent that pollution, and some also save water.

Conventional or ecological?

Neither is always better. A system is only as good as its design, building, use and care, and it must suit the place. Some households and communities will be better served by other options.

Safe containment, treatment, handling and maintenance are essential in every system.

Types of ecological sanitation

Urine-diverting dry toilets

How: urine and faeces are kept apart; faeces are stored dry in a closed chamber.
Advantage: little or no water for flushing.
Upkeep: keep the faeces side dry, add dry cover material, handle with care.
Limits: needs correct use by everyone; stored material is not automatically safe to handle.
Ask a specialist: before building, and before any reuse.

Composting toilets

How: waste and added dry material break down over time.
Advantage: can reduce the amount of waste and may produce a material for further treatment.
Upkeep: manage moisture and air; safe emptying.
Limits: the end product can still carry germs and needs further treatment before any use.
Ask a specialist: on design and on what is safe to do with the product.

Other water-saving systems

How: other designs contain and treat waste in different ways.
Advantage: may suit places where water or space is limited.
Upkeep: depends on the design.
Limits: not every system suits every soil, water level or household.
Ask a specialist: always, before choosing.

Possible benefits

  • Saving water, where the design allows.
  • Less pollution of soil and water when well designed and managed.
  • Protecting water sources.
  • Responsible waste management.
  • Recovering nutrients safely after proper treatment, where permitted.
  • Greater awareness of sustainable sanitation.

Benefits depend on design, building, use, upkeep and local conditions. They are not guaranteed.

ECO-SAN and public health

  • Contain human waste safely and keep it away from drinking water.
  • Avoid contact with untreated waste, especially for children and vulnerable people.
  • Wash hands with soap and clean water after using the toilet and after any cleaning.
  • People who maintain a system should use protective measures such as gloves, boots and washing afterwards.
  • Treat waste properly before any permitted reuse.
  • Follow the environmental health and sanitation requirements that apply here.

A poorly designed or poorly maintained system can harm health and the environment.

In homes and communities

  1. Find out what the local sanitation needs are.
  2. Consider soil, groundwater, flooding and the site.
  3. Choose a system that suits the place and the users.
  4. Make sure construction and containment are done properly.
  5. Agree who looks after the system.
  6. Teach everyone how to use it correctly.
  7. Keep wells and other water sources protected.
  8. Arrange safe treatment and handling of waste.
  9. Get professional guidance before installing or changing a system.

No distances, technical specifications or approval rules are given here. They have not been verified for this location. Ask the appropriate authority and a qualified professional.

Common misconceptions

Community education toolkit

Short messages

• Safe sanitation protects everyone’s water.
• Untreated waste is never fertiliser.
• Wash hands every time.
• A system needs care to stay safe.

Meeting discussion points

• Where do people get drinking water?
• What toilets do we use now?
• What are our soil and flooding conditions?
• Who will look after any new system?
• Who should we ask for advice?

Questions before adopting a system

• Is it suitable for our soil and water level?
• Who designs and approves it?
• Who maintains it, and how often?
• What happens to the waste?
• Is any use of the product permitted?

For schools

• Draw the stages of a safe system.
• Practise handwashing steps.
• Role-play “keep the well safe”.

ECO-SAN: key facts

  • ECO-SAN manages human waste safely, can save water, and in some systems recovers nutrients after proper treatment.
  • Systems differ. Not every system suits every household.
  • Never use untreated human waste on food crops or gardens.
  • Every system needs regular care by trained, protected people.
